## 1. Commercial turbofan flagships

### CFM International LEAP (Safran/GE 50-50 JV)
- Thrust: ~23,000–40,000 lbf class depending on variant (LEAP-1A, -1B, -1C)
- First run: 4 September 2013; EIS on A320neo (2016) and 737 MAX (2017)
- Applications: Airbus A320neo family, Boeing 737 MAX, COMAC C919
- Bypass ratio: ~11:1; fuel burn ~15% better than CFM56
- Innovations: CMC (ceramic matrix composite) turbine shrouds, additive
  manufacturing (3D-printed fuel nozzles), dual-annular pre-swirl combustor
- Sources: [Wikipedia — CFM LEAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_LEAP),
  [CFM RISE](https://www.cfmaeroengines.com/rise)

### Pratt & Whitney PW1000G (geared turbofan / PurePower)
- Thrust: 15,000–33,000 lbf (GTF Advantage raises hot-and-high thrust to
  34,000 lbf / 151 kN, up to +8%)
- Bypass ratio: ~12.5:1; overall pressure ratio up to 42:1
- Applications: A320neo (PW1100G), A220 (PW1500G), E-Jets E2 (PW1700/1900),
  Embraer Praetor (PW1700), Mitsubishi SpaceJet (cancelled)
- Key innovation: fan drive gear system decoupling fan from low-pressure
  spool, enabling ultra-high bypass without a larger core
- Operational notes: powder-metal durability issue drove fleet-wide
  inspections/removals; GTF Advantage (Dec 2021) targets durability and
  +1% fuel efficiency
- Sources: [Wikipedia — PW1000G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney_PW1000G),
  [P&W spec chart (Stanford mirror)](http://large.stanford.edu/courses/2014/ph240/suresh1/docs/pwspecs.pdf),
  [FAA GTF paper](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/about/office_org/headquarters_offices/apl/pratt_whitney_0.pdf)

### GE Aerospace GE9X
- Thrust: certified operating rating ~103,000 lbf; holds a ground-test record
  of ~134,300 lbf (**record is not the certified rating**)
- Fan diameter: 134 in; CMCs in the turbine, 3D-printed parts
- Application: Boeing 777X (sole engine)
- Note: largest commercial engine built; entry into service deferred with
  the 777X schedule
- Sources: [Wikipedia — GE9X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_GE9X)

### GE Aerospace GEnx
- Thrust: ~66,500–78,000 lbf; bypass ratio up to 9.0:1; OPR up to 58.1:1
- Fuel efficiency ~15% better than CF6
- Applications: 787 Dreamliner (plus 747-8 derivative variants)
- Sources: [Wikipedia — GEnx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_GEnx)

### Rolls-Royce Trent XWB
- Thrust: up to 97,000 lbf
- Application: Airbus A350 (sole engine); EIS 2015
- First run: 18 June 2010 (FlightGlobal archive)
- Sources: [Wikipedia — Trent XWB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Trent_XWB),
  [Rolls-Royce product page](https://www.rolls-royce.com/products-and-services/civil-aerospace/widebody/trent-xwb.aspx)

### Others
- IAE V2500 — A320ceo family workhorse (~25,000 lbf)
- GP7200 (Engine Alliance) — A380
- Safran/MTU/PW2000 family — older widebody/narrowbody applications
- COMAC C919 uses LEAP-1C; domestic CJ-1000A (AECC) is the intended replacement

## 2. Regional and business-jet engines

### GE Catalyst (Advanced Turboprop)
- Power: ~850–1,600 shp class; first clean-sheet certified turboprop in
  decades (FAA certification 2025 for Denali application)
- Applications: Cessna Denali, De Havilland DHC-8 re-engine, Pratt &
  Whitney Canada competitor to PT6
- Innovations: 3D-printed components, 16:1 OPR, FADEC, ~20% better fuel burn
  vs PT6A
- Sources: [Wikipedia — GE Catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Catalyst)

### P&WC PW100 family
- Market-dominant regional turboprop (Dash 8 Q400, ATR competing with AE2100)
- PW150 on Dash 8 Q400 (~5,000 shp)
- Sources: [Wikipedia — P&WC PW100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney_Canada_PW100G)

### Business jets
- P&WC PW500/PW600/PW800 series — dominant light/mid-cabin platforms
- GE Passport (20,000 lbf) — Bombardier Global 7500; HF120 — HondaJet
- Rolls-Royce Pearl 10X — Dassault Falcon 10X (latest flagship bizjet engine)
- Williams FJ44/FJ33 — light jet staple (Citation, Phenom 300)
- Sources: [Wikipedia — Williams FJ44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_FJ44)

**SAF / hybrid-electric shift:** all major OEMs now certify SAF blends up to
50% (ASTM D7566); several pursuing 100% SAF compatibility. Demonstrators:
Airbus E-Fan X (cancelled 2020), Eviation Alice (all-electric commuter),
Heart Aerospace ES-30 (hybrid-electric regional, ~30 passengers),
Zunum Aero (defunct).

## 3. Emerging propulsion

| Technology | Developer | Claimed gain | Maturity | Barrier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open rotor / unducted fan | CFM RISE | ~20% fuel burn cut | Demonstrator phase, 2030s target | Noise, blade-out containment, integration |
| Hybrid-electric regional | Heart Aerospace ES-30 | ~50% lower operating cost on short routes | Entry into service target ~2028 | Battery energy density, certification |
| All-electric | Eviation Alice | Zero direct emissions | Certification pending, limited range | Battery specific energy (~250 Wh/kg today) |
| Hydrogen combustion | Universal Hydrogen, Airbus ZEROe | CO2-free thrust | Ground/flight test | Cryogenic storage volume, supply chain |
| Hydrogen fuel cell | ZeroAvia | Zero-emission regional retrofit | Flight testing (Dover-Hydrogen) | Fuel-cell power density, durability |
| High-speed / low-boom | Hermeus (Quarterhorse/Chimera), Boom (Overture/Symphony), NASA X-59 | Mach 2.2+ / quiet supersonic | Quarterhorse flew 2024; Overture first flight pending | Turbine-based combined cycle, economics |
| Turbogenerator | Various | Enables hybrid architectures | Development | Power electronics, thermal management |

- **RISE (CFM/GE-Safran):** open-fan architecture, ~20% better fuel
  efficiency, hybrid-electric assist under study, SAF/hydrogen compatible.
  Timeline targets mid-2030s for a next-generation single-aisle.
- **Hydrogen:** Airbus ZEROe concept studies (turbofan/turboprop/hybrid
  wing-body) target 2035 EIS; ZeroAvia and Universal Hydrogen pursue
  retrofit/hydrogen conversion of existing airframes first.

## 4. Market and regulatory landscape

### Market structure
- Four major Western OEMs dominate large civil engines: CFM (market leader
  by narrowbody volume), Pratt & Whitney, GE Aerospace, Rolls-Royce.
- **Backlog context:** CFM's LEAP order book is the industry anchor; Pratt
  and GE hold large widebody orders (777X, 787, A350). Exact 2025 share
  figures were **not verified** this session — treat any specific % as
  needing confirmation from OEM investor filings.
- **Supply chain:** persistent constraints in castings/forgings (single-source
  superalloy suppliers), skilled labor shortages, and GTF durability-related
  removals limiting available flying hours.

### GTF durability issue (significant current event)
- Powder-metal defects led to fleet-wide inspections and engine removals,
  affecting airlines' capacity plans; Pratt developed a more durable
  combustor design (tone problem fix) targeted for introduction ~September
  (per Wikipedia coverage).
- Operational impact: borescope inspections revealing blocked cooling holes
  in combustor panels, attributed to saltier air environments.

### Regulation driving efficiency
- **ICAO CAEP standards:** successive NOx tightening cycles — CAEP/2 (1993),
  CAEP/4 (1999), CAEP/6 (2005), CAEP/8 (2011). Higher bypass ratios and lean-burn
  / rich-quench-lean combustor designs reduce NOx.
- **CORSIA:** carbon offsetting scheme for international flights, phased
  implementation.
- **EU ETS:** emissions trading for intra-EU aviation.
- **FAA Part 33 / Part 34:** engine certification and emissions rules;
  noise stages (Chapter 14 under ICAO Annex 16) shape airframe/engine
  integration choices.

Sources: [Wikipedia — FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Administration),
[Wikipedia — ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO)
